The sacred agreement

A room where honesty is safe.

Wrapped Up only works if a man can speak without being shamed. We meet with compassion for how lust takes hold, and with honesty about the harm it causes.

You can listen until you are ready to speak. You do not need a polished story, a clean week, or proof that you belong.

A clear word about confidentiality

We treat what is shared in the room with care. Before a man shares, leaders explain the group covenant and the limits of confidentiality, including situations involving abuse, immediate danger, or a legal duty to act.

When failure happens

Failure is
never final.

Your failure is not a surprise to God.

A setback deserves honesty, but it does not erase every faithful choice or put you beyond God’s mercy. What matters now is how you respond.

  1. 01

    Tell the truth quickly

    Contact a brother before shame has time to build another hiding place.

  2. 02

    Make the next hour safe

    Leave isolation, move the device, remove access, and get around people.

  3. 03

    Learn without prosecuting yourself

    Name the trigger, the feeling, and the decision. Take responsibility without feeding shame.

  4. 04

    Return to the room

    You are not disqualified because you need the very place built to help you get back up.

How we meet

Seven movements.
One honest room.

Each gathering follows the same rhythm. It gives us enough structure to be honest and enough room for the Spirit to lead.

You are welcome to come and listen before you share.

  1. 01

    Worship

    Turn toward the Lord

    We begin with a song, a surrendered posture, and prayer. We name Jesus as Lord, ask the Holy Spirit to move, and remember that temptation does not own us.

  2. 02

    Radical confession

    Tell the true thing

    We bring sin, fantasy, temptation, and hidden thoughts into the light without graphic detail or comparison. Truth breaks secrecy. Grace meets us there.

  3. 03

    Accountability

    Name the pattern. Set the guardrail.

    We review the week using the four circles. Where did you live? What moved you inward? Which guardrail held? Which one needs to change today?

  4. 04

    Practice

    Learn what sits under the urge

    We use a feelings wheel, gratitude, and honest reflection to recognize what we are carrying. We practice meeting loneliness, anger, fear, boredom, exhaustion, and stress without turning to lust for relief.

  5. 05

    Prayer

    Stand with a brother

    We pray over one another for forgiveness, healing, courage, and freedom. We ask Christ to break every chain, drive back the work of darkness, and teach us to steward what he restores.

  6. 06

    Expect

    Speak God’s truth

    We answer shame and temptation with Scripture. We remember God’s promises of mercy, renewal, endurance, restoration, and a way through temptation.

  7. 07

    Do

    Keep your word this week

    Each man leaves with a clear commitment. We reach out before secrecy takes over, practice daily gratitude, fast in a wise and healthy way, pray through Ephesians 6, and do what we said we would do.

A tool for the week

See the slide before the fall.

Most men do not move from peace to acting out in one instant. The four circles make the movement visible early enough to choose differently.

Moving inward is not a verdict. It is information. The earlier you tell the truth, the more choices you have.

Move outward↑

Circle 4

Rooted and free

You are connected to God and other men. You are honest, rested, purposeful, and present. Healthy routines and clear boundaries are in place.

Protect it with: prayer, sleep, movement, meaningful work, service, gratitude, device boundaries, and regular contact.

Circle 3

Drifting

Warning signs begin to appear. You may notice isolation, resentment, boredom, stress, fatigue, missed check-ins, or unstructured screen time.

Respond by: naming the feeling, telling a brother, changing your environment, and restoring the routine that keeps you grounded.

Circle 2

Feeding the urge

The mind starts bargaining. Fantasy, secret scrolling, testing guardrails, revisiting old paths, or planning privacy makes the urge stronger.

Respond now: stop, leave the setting, hand over the device, call a brother, pray out loud, and follow your agreed escape plan.

Circle 1

Crossing the line

A boundary has been crossed, or acting out is close. Do not let shame turn one decision into a return to hiding.

Respond without delay: interrupt the behavior, get into contact, tell the truth, protect anyone affected, and make a specific repair and prevention plan.

The weekly review

Bring these questions to the room.

  1. Where did I spend most of the week?
  2. What moved me inward?
  3. What was I feeling or trying to feed?
  4. Which guardrail held?
  5. Which guardrail failed?
  6. What must I remove, and what will take its place?

Build the guardrails

Make the next faithful choice easier.

A guardrail is not punishment. It is a decision made while you are thinking clearly to protect you when you are not.

When the urge calls itself a reward

  1. Pause Give the feeling ten minutes before you obey it.
  2. Name Say out loud what you actually want to feel.
  3. Move Change rooms, go outside, or get around people.
  4. Call Contact a brother before you make a decision.
  5. Choose Use a healthy form of rest or reward that you planned while clear headed.

The 12-week commitment

Stay long enough to see what honesty can grow.

Freedom is rarely built in one emotional night. Give this process twelve honest weeks. Show up, practice the tools between meetings, and tell the truth when a week goes badly.

Weeks 01-04

Bring it into the light

Learn the covenant. Tell your story at your pace. Define your four circles. Put the first guardrails in place.

Weeks 05-08

Practice a new response

Name feelings and patterns. Build gratitude, prayer, wise fasting, rest, and honest connection into real life.

Weeks 09-12

Walk it out under pressure

Reach out sooner. Repair weak guardrails quickly. Build a clear plan for the next season of freedom.

At week 12, ask

Is there fruit?

  • Am I more honest?
  • Do I recognize my patterns sooner?
  • Are my guardrails stronger?
  • Am I quicker to reach out?
  • Is my life becoming more aligned with Christ?

Then decide your next faithful step.

What we stand on

Grace first.
Then a different life.

Jesus holds mercy and change together: “Neither do I condemn you” and “go and sin no more.” Grace is not permission to stay bound. It is the ground under our feet when we repent, get back up, and learn new obedience.

Your cleanest week does not make God love you more. Your worst failure does not put you beyond his reach.

We take spiritual warfare seriously. We also take responsibility for our choices. We do not make peace with lust. We fight under Christ’s authority, with spiritual armor, practical guardrails, and brothers beside us. Shame and isolation do not get the final word.
